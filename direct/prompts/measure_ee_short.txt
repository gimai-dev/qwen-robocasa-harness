- "measure": {"k": "measure", "cam": "left"|"right"|"wrist", "region": [u0, v0, u1, v1], "above_z": <world z in metres or null>, "p": null, "o": null, "g": null, "a": null, "v": null, "n": "..."} (for every other kind set cam, region and above_z to null).
  The simulator returns the 3D statistics of every visible surface point inside that pixel rectangle of that camera, computed from a calibrated depth render taken at the same instant: `centroid_world_m`, `min_world_m`, `max_world_m`, `extent_m`, `top_point_world_m` and `n_points`, all in the WORLD frame (the same frame as your "ee" targets). It consumes no simulator steps and does not move the robot; it costs one decision.
- Pixel convention: u is the column (0 at the left edge, 255 at the right edge), v is the row (0 at the top, 255 at the bottom) of the 256x256 image of that camera; u0 < u1 and v0 < v1. `tcp_pixels` and the `regions` pixel entries use the same convention, so you can build a rectangle around a region's pixel location or around what you see.
- Depth gives the VISIBLE surface. An object's geometric centre is about extent_m[2]/2 below `top_point_world_m` and near the middle of min/max in x and y. A rectangle that also covers the counter top or a far background yields a large extent and a centroid that is not on the object: tighten the rectangle, or set `above_z` to the surface height (e.g. the counter top z) so only points above it count.
- Use it to fix the grasp target before descending: measure the object, set the ee target x, y at the middle of min/max and z about 0.01 to 0.02 m below the top point for a downward grasp, then descend. Remeasure after the arm or base has moved if the estimate matters. At most 6 measurements in a row without moving.
