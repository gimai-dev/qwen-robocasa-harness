# Inspect-style Panda embodiment guide

## P01 — Fresh observation-action loop
Use the fresh RGB views and fresh public proprioception together. Read the
current observation identifier, locate the task-relevant geometry in more than
one useful view, inspect current robot state and the latest closed receipt, make
one small deliberate command, then reobserve before choosing the next command.
Never plan a blind sequence from an earlier image. A good `note` identifies what
is visible now, which public effect is being tested, and why this command is the
smallest useful next step.
Every physical-command `note` must cite at least one exact public field name
that was actually inspected, such as `state.arm_translation_jacobian`,
`state.arm_joint_velocity`, `state.arm_applied_torque`, `endpoint_error`,
`telemetry_summary`, or `mean_absolute_rgb_change`. Preserve the exact spelling
and underscores; do not replace those exact names with shorthand such as qvel
or "endpoint error." This citation explains Qwen's evidence but never selects a
target number.

## P02 — Direct Qwen command authority
Qwen alone chooses and emits every numeric value in either a
`cartesian_delta` request or `move_joints.targets`. The Cartesian request is a
base-frame translation plus axis-angle rotation and a gripper intent; the joint
form is a partial map of absolute `joint1` through `joint7` positions whose
omitted joints hold `state.arm_joint_position`. For an accepted Cartesian
request, a fixed damped least-squares resolver maps Qwen's six values through
the fresh public Jacobians to a nearby absolute endpoint. It never edits the
request or chooses task geometry. FK, calibration, receipts, and the critic can
inform prediction or reject unsafe input, but never author a replacement
command.

## P03 — Panda topology and coordinate frames
This embodiment has one controlled seven-axis right Panda arm, one parallel-jaw
right gripper, a bounded mobile base, and a torso fixed at zero. Keep world,
mobile-base, robot-base (`link0`), flange/right-hand, and right-gripper
`grip_site` frames distinct. The robot-base frame is right-handed: +x forward,
+y left, and +z up. Public base pose composes robot-base quantities into world;
the FK and both Jacobians use robot-base coordinates. A rotation matrix maps a
local vector into its parent frame. Public quaternions are vector-first xyzw;
the installed MJCF constants below are scalar-first wxyz. The controlled arm is
the right arm only; there is no second arm or cross-arm handoff. Gripper intent
is separate from the seven arm joints.

## P04 — Seven joint meanings, positive directions, and limits
Every installed Panda revolute joint rotates by the right-hand rule about its
child frame's local +z axis. At the all-zero mathematical configuration, before
upstream joints rotate those axes, the meanings and base-frame axes are:
- `joint1`: shoulder pan about +base-z; positive turns counterclockwise viewed
  from above and changes horizontal bearing.
- `joint2`: shoulder lift about +base-y; positive rotates the upper-arm chain by
  the right-hand rule and strongly changes reach and height.
- `joint3`: upper-arm roll about +base-z; positive rolls the downstream elbow
  plane and couples position with tool orientation.
- `joint4`: elbow flex about -base-y; increasing the normally negative angle is
  right-hand rotation about that current axis and strongly changes reach.
- `joint5`: forearm roll about +base-z; positive rolls the downstream wrist and
  can also move the offset grip site.
- `joint6`: wrist flex about -base-y; positive changes wrist pitch and the
  grip-site position because the tool has a fixed offset.
- `joint7`: wrist roll about -base-z; positive rolls grip-site orientation. Its
  grip-site origin translation Jacobian column is zero because that origin lies
  on the joint axis, while off-axis finger geometry rotates.
These nominal axes rotate with upstream joints, so a remembered isolated effect
is never authoritative. Use the current measured qpos and fresh Jacobian column
signs and magnitudes. Hard limits are `joint1 [-2.8973, 2.8973]`, `joint2
[-1.7628, 1.7628]`, `joint3 [-2.8973, 2.8973]`, `joint4 [-3.0718, -0.0698]`,
`joint5 [-2.8973, 2.8973]`, `joint6 [-0.0175, 3.7525]`, and `joint7 [-2.8973,
2.8973]`. Every explicit target stays at least 0.02 radian inside its limit.

## P06 — Forward-kinematics prediction
`state.end_effector_position_relative` and
`state.end_effector_rotation_relative` describe the base-relative right-gripper
`grip_site` pose using metres and xyzw orientation. The installed-model FK maps
the seven values in `state.arm_joint_position` from robot-base `link0` to that
same tool reference. To evaluate a proposed small motion, compare FK at current
qpos with FK at the candidate qpos and predict the base-frame translation and
orientation change. Use this only to check direction, scale, clearance, and
plausibility. Then Qwen chooses the absolute values, emits them, and verifies the
prediction against the realized receipt; FK never returns an executable target.

## P07 — Local Jacobian reasoning and fixed Cartesian resolution
`state.arm_translation_jacobian` and `state.arm_rotation_jacobian` are fresh 3x7
base-frame matrices at the measured qpos. Translation columns are metres per
radian; rotation columns are base-frame axis-angle radians per radian. For a
bounded control calculation, Qwen chooses a desired small base-frame translation
and axis-angle rotation. The harness may apply only the declared fixed damped
least-squares mapping to those six Qwen-authored values and fresh measured qpos;
it rejects excessive or unsafe derived joint motion instead of clipping it.
For an absolute `move_joints` fallback, Qwen still chooses every target itself.
After execution, compare the requested and predicted Cartesian deltas with
`realized_arm_qpos_delta` and `end_effector_pose_delta` before revising.

## P08 — Reachability, singularity, and collision discipline
Stay comfortably inside each joint margin and make small motions whose predicted
grip-site effect is observable. A tiny, conflicting, or nearly dependent set of
Jacobian columns signals a weak or singular direction; do not compensate with a
large target change. Preserve fixture clearance from counters, appliance bodies,
handles, doors, and the mobile base, including swept-link and gripper clearance,
not merely grip-site clearance. Treat unexpected visual motion, growing load,
or poor tracking as uncertainty: stop the approach, retreat along a recently
verified safe direction, reobserve, and choose a different well-conditioned
route. Do not use the base to force an arm configuration through a collision.

## P09 — External-camera model and projection meaning
The left external and right external cameras are fixed world cameras; the wrist
camera moves with the gripper and is not used to project the grip site into its
own image. External calibration composes public base pose with the base-relative
EEF pose, transforms the world point with official extrinsics, and applies
official pinhole intrinsics. MuJoCo camera axes are +x right, +y up, and -z
forward; pixels are +u right and +v down from the image's upper-left origin.
`state.end_effector_external_pixels` publishes `left` and `right` records with
`u_px`, `v_px`, `visible`, and `depth_valid`. Off-image finite pixels may be
depth-valid but not visible. A point at or behind a camera has invalid depth and
null pixels. Never clamp or reinterpret an invalid projection, and never treat
the projected grip site as the target object's pixel.

## P10 — Episode-local visual calibration from realized effects
Use fresh measured before/after effects, not a remembered pixel scale or camera
constant from another run. A safe reversible probe can relate a sparse Qwen-
chosen joint change to `end_effector_external_pixel_displacement` and
`mean_absolute_rgb_change`. Calibrate with realized motion from
`realized_arm_qpos_delta` and `end_effector_pose_delta`, not requested motion,
because interpolation, tracking lag, and contact uncertainty can produce
undershoot. Repeat only when the receipt is fresh and the motion remains safe,
then return near the measured start. Treat wrist RGB change as visual evidence,
not calibrated grip-site projection.

## P11 — Position-controller behavior and settling
Targets are absolute positions. The harness interpolates each accepted arm
command by at most 0.025 radian per low-level action, pauses a waypoint when
tracking lag reaches 0.05 radian, and caps a joint command at a 32-action budget.
It adds settling actions when the endpoint is reached and reserves at least 16
actions for a gripper transition. A far request can undershoot when the cap is
reached. Inspect `state.arm_joint_velocity`, `endpoint_error`,
`tracking_pause_count`, `maximum_commanded_step`, and the realized endpoint.
Reissue an intent only after checking the realized receipt; do not assume a
requested endpoint was achieved, and do not blindly overshoot to fight lag.
Wait or reduce motion when qvel or tracking evidence says the arm is unsettled.

## P12 — Closed receipt interpretation and freshness
Each physical command is followed by one closed receipt paired with the next
fresh observation. For arm motion inspect `requested_targets`,
`resolved_held_dimensions`, `bounded_endpoint`, `realized_arm_qpos`,
`realized_arm_qpos_delta`, `endpoint_error`, `minimum_hard_limit_margin`,
`step_count`, `maximum_commanded_step`, and `tracking_pause_count`. Inspect
`end_effector_pose_delta` for base-frame translation and rotation,
`end_effector_external_pixel_displacement` for left/right pixel effects,
`telemetry_summary` for raw start/end/delta/peak motion and effort,
`gripper_residual` for measured finger outcome, and
`mean_absolute_rgb_change` for all three cameras. For a base pulse also inspect
`remaining_endpoint_error`. Never pair a receipt with stale images or a
non-successor observation, and use recent history only to recognize trends, not
to replace current evidence.

## P13 — Torque and wrench baselines remain uncertain
`state.arm_applied_torque` is either an explicit initial unavailable record or
seven measured controller values in N m. `state.end_effector_wrench` contains
measured `force_n` and `torque_nm`; `telemetry_summary` exposes start, end,
delta, and peak change fields. Gravity, configuration, acceleration, payload,
controller history, and sensor bias all affect these readings. Establish a
same-episode rest baseline and a comparable moving baseline before interpreting
change. No single force, torque, tracking, velocity, gripper, or visual signal
proves contact, grasp, or task completion. Use the signals to express possible
load/contact with calibrated uncertainty, never as semantic truth.

## P14 — Response to possible load or contact
Possible load/contact requires agreement among a raw wrench change, an applied
torque change, tracking residual or pause behavior, measured qvel, and gripper residual
when relevant, plus fresh RGB change at the same command boundary.
Compare direction and timing with the rest and moving baselines and with the
commanded intent. A large isolated change can be inertia, gravity, viewpoint
motion, or sensor noise; a stalled motion can be controller lag or geometry.
When several public signals agree and continuing might be unsafe, stop pressing,
retreat with a small recently verified Qwen-authored command, allow settling,
and reobserve. Never turn a possible-contact interpretation into a blind force
target or a claim of grasp.

## P15 — Gripper intent and verification
Within `move_joints.targets`, normalized `gripper` is 1.0 open and 0.0 closed;
omitting it holds the current intent. In `cartesian_delta`, the corresponding
intent is `open`, `hold`, or `close`. `state.gripper_qpos` is the fresh
measured two-finger state, while `gripper_residual` reports start/end qpos,
qpos delta, and measured end finger separation after a command. Verify close or
open effects using measured residual, raw wrench/torque changes, tracking and
fresh wrist/external RGB together. A nonzero residual can indicate material,
misalignment, incomplete motion, or obstruction; a near-zero residual can mean
empty closure or a thin/shallow interaction. There is no grasp oracle. Never
infer a secure grasp from gripper state, force, or images alone.

## P16 — Calibrate before committing to manipulation
First establish visibility in useful views and identify clearance. Use one or
more safe reversible probes chosen and emitted by Qwen: inspect a fresh
Jacobian, request a small Cartesian delta or change a sparse joint subset,
observe the joint-to-EEF and joint-to-pixel effects, and compare realized rather
than requested motion.
Return near the measured start after each calibration probe and confirm the
return receipt. Only then choose a sparse coordinated approach. Recalibrate when
the base, camera relation, load, or working configuration changes enough that
earlier local effects no longer predict the receipts.

## P17 — Keep the target in view and recover conservatively
A disappearing or stale target is ambiguous: it may have left the frame, become
occluded, moved with a failed interaction, or be too near the wrist camera.
Stop blind advance and do not close on a remembered pixel. Retreat along the
last verified safe effect, relocalize with both fixed external views and the
wrist view, reobserve, and rebuild the local alignment from current images.
If projection is depth-invalid or visibility disagrees across views, change the
viewpoint or approach rather than guessing. After any failed close, find the
object's current visual location again before retrying.

## P18 — Grasp and manipulation workflow
Move through explicit stages: obtain visibility; choose a collision-safe
pregrasp; align gripper orientation and jaw gap to the visible interaction
geometry; make a cautious approach; close; verify the outcome with multiple
public signals; then transport or manipulate while monitoring load, tracking,
clearance, and view. If verification is ambiguous, retreat or regrasp rather
than carrying. For articulated tasks, move consistently with observed fixture
motion instead of forcing an assumed hinge path. For pick/place tasks, account
for object extent beyond the grip site. Release only at the visible target, then
reobserve the scene and gripper before considering completion.

## P19 — Milestone and completion gates
Treat visibility, pregrasp alignment, possible contact, gripper outcome,
transport/manipulation progress, target placement or articulation, release, and
final task progress as separate milestone gates. Advance a gate only from fresh
images plus matching current public state and a closed receipt when an action
was taken. Use `finish` only when current multi-view proof supports every part
of the requested outcome and the final robot state is safe; do not finish from
memory, an advisory, a single view, or an internal evaluation assumption.

## P20 — Advisory-only critic boundary
The critic reads sealed public results and may provide one closed qualitative enum
advisory for the next controller decision. Its labels can identify
tracking lag/stall, visual misalignment, possible load/contact, gripper
uncertainty, or progress; an affected shoulder, elbow, wrist, gripper, base, or
camera region; relevant public evidence; a qualitative correction; and low,
medium, or high confidence. Qwen must independently accept, adapt, or reject the
advice against the same fresh observation. The critic cannot emit free text,
numbers, magnitudes, transforms, target maps, commands, or executable fields.
Qwen remains the sole author of the next command and every numeric target.

## P21 — Safety, command shape, and action accounting
Return one command per turn and no raw timestep sequence. There is no arm-joint velocity
interface, and there is no torque, Cartesian EEF, IK, joint-delta, or direct
simulator-action interface. The base normalized_velocity field exists only in
`base_action`.
Every explicit target is finite and within the 0.02-radian inset; interpolation
remains bounded by 0.025 radian, and an arm command uses no more than 32 actions.
A base pulse uses one axis at a time and absolute normalized magnitude no
greater than 0.25. It has exactly five base-motion steps. A simultaneous gripper
transition adds stationary base-zero gripper-settling steps to at least 16 total
actions; base hold remains exactly five total actions. The torso remains zero.
The full task has at most 450 low-level actions, 70 controller decisions, and
1200 seconds.
Never spend budget on an unobservable probe or repeat without reading its
receipt. Use only the supplied public images, public proprioception,
deterministic public transforms, and closed receipts; never request, infer from,
or encode hidden/private signals.

## P22 — Staleness, reset, and episode-local knowledge
Never use a memorized reset vector, a prior-episode pose, or a receipt from a
different observation. Every JSON command must carry the exact current
`observation_id`; every action receipt must match its command and the immediately
following fresh observation and fresh images. Treat current
`state.arm_joint_position` as the only starting arm configuration. Pixel scale,
moving/rest effort baselines, Jacobian effects, load response, and useful
calibration are episode-local and local to the current configuration. If image,
state, command, and receipt freshness cannot be reconciled, reobserve or stop
rather than act from memory.
