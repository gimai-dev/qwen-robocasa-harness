# Embodiment notes

One controlled seven-axis Franka Emika Panda right arm with a parallel-jaw
gripper on an Omron mobile base; the torso is fixed at zero and there is no
second arm. The robot-base frame is right-handed: +x forward, +y left, +z up.
Public FK, both Jacobians, `cartesian_delta`, and every receipt use that
frame. Public quaternions are xyzw.
Joint guide (positive direction, absolute radians, hard limits in brackets):
- `joint1` [-2.8973, 2.8973]: base pan about +z; positive swings the whole arm
  counterclockwise seen from above and sets the horizontal bearing.
- `joint2` [-1.7628, 1.7628]: shoulder lift; positive leans the upper arm
  forward (+x) and lowers the wrist; negative leans it back and raises it.
- `joint3` [-2.8973, 2.8973]: upper-arm roll; rotates the elbow plane out of
  the vertical bearing plane and couples position with tool orientation.
- `joint4` [-3.0718, -0.0698]: elbow; always negative. Toward -0.07 the arm
  straightens (longer reach); toward -3.07 the forearm folds back.
- `joint5` [-2.8973, 2.8973]: forearm roll; rolls the wrist assembly and moves
  the offset grip site slightly.
- `joint6` [-0.0175, 3.7525]: wrist flex; with `joint2 - joint4 - joint6 = 0`
  the tool points straight down, and increasing `joint6` pitches the tool
  forward toward +x.
- `joint7` [-2.8973, 2.8973]: wrist roll about the tool axis; it rolls the
  finger gap and does not move the grip site (its translation Jacobian column
  is zero).
- `gripper`: normalized 1.0 open, 0.0 closed; `open|hold|close` intent inside
  `cartesian_delta` and `image_servo`.
Every explicit joint target stays at least 0.02 rad inside its limit.
Proportions: shoulder axis 0.333 m above the base; upper arm 0.3266 m (0.316 m
with a 0.0825 m elbow offset); forearm 0.3928 m (0.384 m with the opposite
0.0825 m offset); wrist-flex axis to `grip_site` 0.2217 m (0.088 m offset plus
0.2035 m along the tool axis). Reach from the shoulder to the wrist axis is at
most 0.7194 m. `grip_site` is the kinematic reference of
`state.end_effector_position_relative`; it sits between the fingers, not at
the fingertips, and is never the target object's location.

# Rig facts

## Gripper
- `gripper_residual.measured_end_finger_separation` reads about 0.079 m fully
  open and about 0.0024 m when the fingers close on nothing. Contact requires
  a settled value strictly greater than 0.0032 m; in between is empty. A first
  obstruction above 0.0032 m is provisional while finger `qpos_delta` exceeds
  0.002; one stationary close-only repeat confirms or clears it.
- The fingers close along `grip_site` local +y. In the base frame that axis
  is the second column of the rotation given by
  `state.end_effector_rotation_relative`. `joint7` rolls that axis about the
  tool axis; a change of about pi/2 in `joint7` swaps it with local +x. A
  thin bar is held only when the gap axis crosses the bar's long axis; when
  the gap is parallel to the bar the fingers slide past it and the close reads
  empty even with a perfect pixel.

## Cameras and pixels
- `left` and `right` are fixed external cameras; `wrist` moves with the
  gripper. Images are 256 px square and carry the 32-pixel grid; `[0,0]` is
  the upper-left corner, `u` right, `v` down. To read a pixel, count the
  yellow vertical lines to the left of the feature (32 px each) and add the
  fraction of the next cell for `u`; count cyan horizontal lines above it for
  `v`. A target pixel always comes from a visible feature you have located
  this way; `[128,128]` is the image centre, not a target.
- `state.end_effector_external_pixels` publishes the projected `grip_site`
  for each external camera as `u_px`, `v_px`, `visible`, `depth_valid`. It is
  where the gripper is, never where the target is. `image_servo` steers that
  pixel toward your `target_pixel`; insertion is allowed once they are within
  8 pixels.
- Each accepted `image_servo` moves along the camera ray in `step_m`
  increments of 0.005 to 0.03 m, at most four increments and 0.08 m per
  command (0.15 m with a stereo target: give both pixels with `step_m=0.03`
  and keep the SAME pixel pair every command until the grip-site pixel is
  within 8 px; changing the pair moves the 3D point and wastes commands); `articulation_motion` uses one increment. Positive `depth_delta_m`
  moves away from the selected camera, negative toward it.
- Pixel scale at the working depth is roughly 3 to 5 pixels per centimetre in
  the external views (a 3.4 cm grip-site move displaced the right-view pixel
  by 12 px). It changes with depth; calibrate it from
  `end_effector_external_pixel_displacement` against
  `end_effector_pose_delta` in your own receipts, never from memory.
- Thin dark horizontal pulls on appliance doors read as a dark bar a few
  tens of px long and under 10 px tall in the external views; the door panel
  around them is broad and lighter. Put a handle pixel on the interior
  midline of the dark bar, not on the panel, gap, or end cap: from a box
  `u_min..u_max, v_min..v_max` take `u = (u_min + u_max) / 2` and
  `v = (v_min + v_max) / 2`. A pixel within 8 px of either end is rejected
  as an end cap, and a close there misses the bar. When a handle pixel is
  rejected, the rejection names the `u`/`v` span of the dark bar it detected
  in that view; choose your next pixel at the midpoint of that span.
- Several thin bars are usually detected per view. The door pull sits on the
  door face at roughly mid-height of the appliance; a bar along the
  appliance's bottom edge is its base or foot, a bar along the top edge is
  normally the lid seam, and distant bars are counter or cabinet edges.
  In the seed-7 scene the pull is the bar whose vertical position is between
  the appliance's top seam and its base, in both views at the same height.
- An open gripper whose measured finger separation drops below 0.075 m during
  an insertion is being pushed by the handle: that obstruction counts as
  insertion-ready, so a stationary close is allowed even when the selected
  view's pixel error is still above 8 px.
- `other_view_pixel` must be the OBJECT seen in the other camera, never the
  published grip-site pixel of that camera (that is where the gripper is).
  If you cannot find the object in the other view, use `null`.
- One view is not enough for depth. Alignment of the projected grip site with
  the pull in one external view fixes two coordinates; the third (depth along
  that camera's ray) is only confirmed when the grip site also lies on the
  pull in the other external view (`state.end_effector_external_pixels`).
  In the seed-7 episodes both empty closes happened with the grip site within
  3 px of the pull in the right view and more than 80 px off it in the left
  view: the fingers closed above and in front of the bar. The direct fix is a
  stereo target: give `target_pixel` in the selected view and
  `other_view_pixel` for the same point in the other view, and the harness
  intersects the two rays. Close only when both views agree.

## Controller behaviour
- Targets are absolute positions. The harness interpolates at most 0.025 rad
  per low-level action, pauses when tracking lag reaches 0.05 rad, and caps an
  arm command at 32 actions; a gripper transition reserves at least 16
  actions. A far request undershoots when the cap is hit: read
  `endpoint_error`, `tracking_pause_count`, `maximum_commanded_step`, and
  `realized_arm_qpos_delta`, then re-issue the same target rather than
  overshooting.
- A `joint7` roll is settled only when the receipt shows position error at or
  below 0.05 rad and end `joint7` velocity below 0.01 rad/s. One roll reached
  0.047 rad of error while still moving at 0.17 rad/s; that is not settled.
  Repeat the exact same Qwen-authored target until it settles; do not pick a
  new target or resume image servo first.
- In the seed-7 toaster episodes an `image_servo` step used about 18 actions
  and moved the grip site 2 to 3.5 cm; a joint command used 16 to 32 actions.
  A 450-action episode therefore holds roughly 14 to 24 physical commands, so
  an approach that needs more than about 10 servo steps will not leave room
  for engagement and actuation.

## Effort and wrench
- `state.arm_applied_torque` is seven controller torques in N m and is
  gravity-loaded: in the seed-7 approach pose `joint2` held about 14, `joint3`
  about 7, and `joint4` about 9 N m at rest. Compare deltas against the
  moving baseline of your own recent receipts, not against zero.
- `state.end_effector_wrench` gives `force_n` and `torque_nm`;
  `telemetry_summary` reports start, end, delta, and peak per command for
  torque, joint position, joint velocity, and wrench.
- An empty stationary close changed every joint torque by less than 0.6 N m
  and the wrench force by less than 0.05 N. That is the no-contact baseline: a
  real handle contact shows a finger separation above 0.0032 m together with a
  wrench delta clearly above it. No single signal proves contact; require
  agreement between the residual, the wrench, and fresh RGB change.

## Receipts
- Every physical command returns one sealed receipt paired with the next
  observation. Read `requested_targets` or the requested Cartesian/servo
  fields, `bounded_endpoint`, `realized_arm_qpos`, `realized_arm_qpos_delta`,
  `end_effector_pose_delta`, `end_effector_external_pixel_displacement`,
  `endpoint_error`, `minimum_hard_limit_margin`, `step_count`,
  `gripper_residual`, `telemetry_summary`, and `mean_absolute_rgb_change`.
  For a base pulse read `remaining_endpoint_error`.
- Receipts are episode-local: never reuse a pose, pixel scale, or torque
  baseline from another episode, and never act on a receipt that does not
  belong to the current `observation_id`.

# Rig formulas

Planar arm kinematics in the vertical plane through the base bearing, from
the installed Panda model with `joint1 = joint3 = joint5 = joint7 = 0`.
Constants: L0 = 0.333 (shoulder height), L1 = 0.3266, a1 = 0.2554,
L2 = 0.3928, a2 = 0.2116, L3 = 0.2217, a3 = 0.4081 (metres and radians).
Angles are measured from +z toward +x (forward):
- A = joint2 + a1 (upper arm), B = joint2 - joint4 - a2 (forearm),
  phi = joint2 - joint4 - joint6 (tool pitch: 0 points straight down, -pi/2
  points forward), C = phi + pi - a3 (wrist-to-grip-site segment).
- Forward: x = L1 sin A + L2 sin B + L3 sin C and
  z = L0 + L1 cos A + L2 cos B + L3 cos C. Rotate (x, z) by `joint1` about +z
  for the base-frame position; this reproduces the installed FK exactly in
  the planar configuration.
- Inverse for a target (x, z, phi): W = (x, z) - L3 (sin C, cos C);
  P = W - (0, L0); d = |P| must satisfy d <= L1 + L2 = 0.7194; then
  A = atan2(P_x, P_z) - acos((d^2 + L1^2 - L2^2) / (2 L1 d)),
  joint2 = A - a1, B = atan2(W_x - L1 sin A, W_z - L0 - L1 cos A),
  joint4 = joint2 - a2 - B, joint6 = joint2 - joint4 - phi. This minus sign
  is the elbow-back branch the Panda uses; it gives a legal negative `joint4`
  in nearly every working pose, and the plus branch is the alternative when it
  does not.
- Accuracy: exact in the planar configuration. With the real out-of-plane
  values from the seed-7 approach (`joint3 = -0.33`, `joint5 = -0.21`) the
  planar estimate was 10 cm off, so use the formulas for direction, scale,
  reachability, and a first joint set, then correct with the fresh public
  Jacobian and the realized receipt. For an accepted `cartesian_delta` the
  harness already applies one fixed damped least-squares solve of your six
  base-frame values through `state.arm_translation_jacobian` and
  `state.arm_rotation_jacobian`; predict the effect from the same matrices.
- Finger-gap orientation: read the current gap axis from the second column of
  `state.end_effector_rotation_relative`; to cross a horizontal bar that runs
  along base y while approaching from above, the gap must lie near base x.
  With `joint7 = -0.24` in the seed-7 approach pose the gap read near
  `[0.88, 0.38, 0.29]`. Choose the absolute `joint7` target yourself with
  at least 0.02 rad of limit margin.

# Rig advice

Working practice distilled from the seed-7 toaster episodes on this rig.

## Calibrate before you commit
- The episode starts with the arm still settling: a first `move_joints` with
  only `gripper` lets `state.arm_joint_velocity` reach zero and gives a clean
  torque and wrench baseline in the first receipt.
- One small reversible `cartesian_delta` or the first `image_servo` step
  calibrates pixel scale: divide `end_effector_external_pixel_displacement`
  by the realized `end_effector_pose_delta`, not by the requested motion.
- Prefer a coordinated sparse joint set from the planar solution over nudging
  single joints when the target is far; prefer `image_servo` once the target
  and the projected grip site share an external view.

## Keep the target in view
- Cross-check the handle in both external views and name the local visible
  part under the pixel in the note. If the projected grip-site pixel is off
  image, depth-invalid, or the target is occluded, retreat with a bounded open
  `cartesian_delta` and relocalize from fresh RGB; never close on a remembered
  pixel.
- Once chosen, keep the same camera and `target_pixel` fixed across servo
  steps until its stationary close tests contact; local drift of the grip-site
  pixel is not relocalization.

## Approach geometry for a pull on a vertical face
- A pull on a door face cannot be reached from above: with the tool pointing
  down the gripper body meets the appliance's top edge first. In the seed-7
  episodes the stereo servo converged to the right pixel and then stalled 18
  px above the bar with under 5 mm realized per command. Come as
  horizontally as the arm allows: a full -pi/2 tool pitch is NOT reachable
  here (it needs joint6 beyond its 3.75 rad limit at radii of 0.35 to 0.55 m);
  phi = joint2 - joint4 - joint6 of about -1.0 to -1.2 rad (tool 30 to 20
  degrees below horizontal, approach axis near [0.9, 0, -0.4]) is reachable
  with joint6 near 2.7 to 3.1 rad. Put the finger gap vertical (second
  column of `state.end_effector_rotation_relative` near +-z), then approach
  along +x slightly downward.
- Only after a sealed toaster front-pull approach stalls within 20 pixels,
  probe the door top edge already between the fingers: author a stationary
  close now with only `gripper: 0.0`; do not repeat, do not re-orient, and do
  not move the arm first. If contact is confirmed, first actuate with a
  zero-depth downward image waypoint within 16 px and inspect its receipt.
- Wrist rolls are expensive on this rig: `joint7` tracks at only about
  0.3 rad/s, so a quarter turn costs about four 32-action commands (a
  third of the episode). Set the finger gap you want in the ready pose
  (one command) and avoid later rolls; for the seed-7 pull, which runs
  along base y, the default gap near base x already straddles it, so do
  not roll unless a close has proven the gap wrong.
- Only after the harness reports `APPROACH_STALL_CONTEXT` (or after an
  empty close with both views agreeing) author the re-orientation as one
  `move_joints` from the planar formulas: keep the current radius and height,
  solve `joint2`, `joint4`, `joint6` for phi = -1.1 at your own r and z
  (a pitch of -0.4 is not a re-orientation: the tool still points down and
  the same stall repeats), check every value is 0.02 rad inside its limit,
  then choose `joint7` for a vertical gap, gripper `1.0`. Do not re-orient during the approach; the
  stereo servo is cheaper while the grip site is still moving. Expect
  undershoot; re-issue the same targets once if the receipt shows tracking
  error above 0.05 rad. A `joint7` change of a quarter turn costs about three
  32-action commands on this rig; budget for it.
- When two consecutive servos toward one target realize under 6 mm, stop
  servoing; the harness marks the approach stalled and rejects a repeat.
- Ready pose first: the episode starts with the arm still moving and often
  nearly straight (`joint4` above -0.5 rad). A straight arm cannot move
  forward-and-down, so before the first servo author one `move_joints` to a
  bent ready pose that keeps reach in every direction, about `joint2 -0.5`,
  `joint4 -1.5`, `joint6 1.5`, gripper `1.0`. A joint command realizes at
  most about 0.5 rad per 32-action command, so this pose takes two commands;
  do not chase the exact target beyond that, start the stereo servo as soon
  as `joint4` is below -1.0. Skip the ready pose entirely if `joint4` is
  already below -1.0.
- Reach: when `joint4` rises above about -0.3 rad the arm is nearly straight
  and no Cartesian or servo command can extend it further (the rejection
  names `joint4 endpoint is unsafe`). Gain reach with one `base_action`
  (`x` forward at +0.25 for five steps moves the base a few centimetres; it
  costs only 5 actions), then re-issue the same stereo target. Do this early
  when the standing target is more than 0.5 m from the grip site.
- The grip site sits about 5 cm above the fingertips along the tool axis;
  a stereo target on the pull therefore parks the fingertips just below the
  bar. Both stereo pixels must always name the SAME bar in both views (the
  pull, at the same height in both images); never pair the pull in one view
  with the base line in the other, that pair is rejected every time.
- While a stereo servo is still converging (the receipt shows motion and the
  grip-site pixel outside 8 px), the next decision is limited to another
  stereo `image_servo`; Cartesian lifts and retreats are not offered.
- After your first consistent stereo pair the harness publishes
  `STANDING_STEREO_TARGET`: that 3D point projected into both fresh views.
  Near the handle the bar detector often loses the pull behind the gripper,
  so keep re-issuing those projected pixels (exempt from the pull gate)
  instead of re-reading the bars, until the grip-site pixel is within 8 px.

## Engaging a thin pull
- Align the grip-site pixel within 8 px of a midline pixel on the dark bar,
  insert with positive depth and the gripper `open`, then close without arm
  motion (`move_joints` with only `targets={"gripper":0.0}`).
- Before closing, check the other external view: if its grip-site pixel is
  off the pull, keep the pixel and servo depth (nonzero `depth_delta_m`,
  positive away from the camera) until the other view's pixel lands on the
  pull; read `end_effector_external_pixel_displacement` for that view in each
  receipt and flip the sign if the offset grows.
- Read the close receipt. A separation above 0.0032 m with a wrench change is
  contact: confirm with one stationary close-only repeat, then actuate. A
  separation near 0.0024 m with both views agreeing means the gap was
  parallel to the bar, not that the pixel was wrong. Never advance from
  `engage` to `actuate` after this empty close: keep `milestone=engage`, reopen
  before retrying, and retreat with one bounded open `cartesian_delta`; then
  author `move_joints` with only `joint7` and
  `gripper=1.0` to roll the gap across the bar, repeat that exact target until
  the receipt shows it settled, and re-insert at a freshly screened pixel.
  Hunting for a second pixel on a short bar wastes revisions.
- A settled roll starts a new handle-contact epoch: earlier empty closes
  explain the roll and no longer demand another retreat.

## Pressing a button or switch (control tasks)
- With `COFFEE_CONTACT_CORRECTION_READY_CONTEXT`, advance to `actuate` and
  author one zero-depth stereo `image_servo`, gripper open, exact schema note.
  Put each pixel 4.5-5.5 px left and 5-6 px down from its projection. This
  correction is the press; inspect its force receipt, then retreat.
- A button is pressed, not grasped. Align the grip site with the control
  within 8 px, close to form a probe (about 0.0024 m is expected), advance to
  `milestone=actuate`, then prefer a positive-depth `image_servo` of 0.01 to
  0.03 m on the same `control_target` with gripper `close` to press along the
  selected camera ray. Use bounded `cartesian_delta` only when the approach
  direction is clear. Verify wrench or appliance RGB effect, then retreat.
  For a knob or lever use `rotation_axis_angle_rad` or a tangential
  translation while keeping contact.

## Actuating
- After confirmed contact keep the gripper `hold` or `close` and trace the
  visible mechanism motion with `image_servo` `target_role=articulation_motion`:
  first waypoint at most 16 px from the current grip-site pixel,
  `depth_delta_m=0.0`, `step_m` 0.01 to 0.02, then read the receipt; once it
  shows the mechanism moved with contact kept, use `step_m=0.03` per command
  (a door needs 10 to 20 cm of handle travel and the action budget is finite).
  Do not merely reverse the engagement ray; the camera ray is not the hinge
  tangent. If contact is lost, re-engage and change the active axis set rather
  than flipping a sign.

## Notes and evidence
- Keep every note under 320 characters (about 50 words); a longer note is
  rejected without execution and a response that runs past the token limit
  voids the whole episode.
- Begin every note with `milestone=NAME; `, cite at least one exact public
  field name you inspected (`state.arm_joint_velocity`,
  `state.arm_translation_jacobian`, `state.arm_applied_torque`,
  `endpoint_error`, `telemetry_summary`, `mean_absolute_rgb_change`, or
  `gripper_residual`), state the predicted effect, and say what receipt value
  would prove it wrong. Numbers in the note explain evidence; the command
  fields carry the authored values.
- Use only public images, public state, deterministic public transforms, and
  sealed receipts; never request or infer hidden simulator truth.
